# Residual anomaly search, first pass: nuclear binding energies and alpha-decay energies

Generated by `residual_analyzer.py` (seed 20261116, Z >= 82, 2000 look-elsewhere simulations per search). Every number below is read from `out/results.json`.

## Verdict

- **No anomaly.** Of 3,824 candidate features in the alpha-decay energies and 3,219 in the binding energies, none reaches 3 sigma in every mass model. Only 1 and 1 reach even 2 sigma in every model.
- **The paper's window shows no signal in any model.** For a charge switched on at (Z, N) = (114, 184), the fitted amplitude sits between -2.3 and +0.1 sigma across the four models that reach Z = 118 and the four widths the paper quotes.
- **The paper's ceiling is robust to the choice of mass model.** At width 5 the 95% ceiling on the central strength is 8.0e13 with the paper's model and 6.7e13 to 8.8e13 across the four models (-16% to +11%). Over widths 4.5 to 10 it spans 4.2e13 to 1.0e14.
- **Nuclear energies cannot see the charge in plutonium or curium.** The largest shift the paper's window still allows in the mass of Pu-244 is 5.7e-28 keV, against a measurement good to 2.3 keV. Only a force measurement reaches it.
- **Atomic spectroscopy cannot see it either.** The force between an electron and a Pu-244 nucleus would shift optical frequencies by at most 1.9e-34 of themselves, 5e15 times below the best optical clocks.

The strongest shared feature in the alpha energies is Ds-273 (Z = 110, N = 163) with its neighbours: high at N = 163 to 164, low at Hs-270 (N = 162). That is the pattern expected if every model slightly underestimates the deformed shell gap at N = 162, a known feature of this region. The models' residuals are only weakly correlated with each other (-0.01 to +0.45 for alpha energies, -0.35 to +0.36 for binding energies), which is what makes the cross-model test informative.

Check: with the paper's model this code reproduces the paper's limits to a ratio of 5e-14 (n = 484).

Scan of where an island could sit instead (1,944 centre and width combinations, Z 100 to 126, N 150 to 196): the best single-model excesses are WS-Ma2019 2.7 sigma (p = 0.40), WS4 1.5 sigma (p = 1.00), FRDM2012 3.5 sigma (p = 0.04), KTUY05 2.3 sigma (p = 0.72). They sit at different places. Requiring all four models, the best location reaches only 1.0 sigma (Z = 112, N = 170), and none reaches 2.

The binding-energy channel adds nothing here: measured masses stop at Z = 110, too far from the island (its ceilings are 4.4e14 or weaker).

The mass models are good to about 389 keV near the island, and that, not the measurements, sets the ceiling. For the superheavy nuclides the allowed shift is comparable to the model error, which is why they constrain the charge at all. For plutonium and curium it is smaller by more than twenty orders of magnitude: the self-energy goes as the square of the nuclide's own charge, which is tiny that far from the island. A torsion balance measures the product of that charge with ordinary matter's, which is why it is the only probe that reaches these nuclides.

## 7. Limits of this pass

- The single-model p-values assume the fitted noise model is right. Section 2 shows it is not near shell closures, so they overstate significance. The cross-model test of section 3 does not rely on them.
- The cross-model score is a screen, not a p-value: models share physics, so their errors are not fully independent.
- The noise model is fitted to the same residuals that are searched, which makes the search conservative for features about as wide as the fitted correlation length (1.3 to 3.9 nucleons).
- FRDM2012 comes from the text of the arXiv PDF (two decimals in MeV). Each row was checked against its own binding-energy column and all 9,318 published rows passed.
- Charge radii and isotope shifts are not ingested. Section 6 explains why that is not a priority.
